\section{Problem and track relevance}

In August 2023 NewsGuard gave 43 AI-rewritten articles from content farms to Grammarly's premium plagiarism checker. It could not find the original source of 34 of them, a 79\% failure rate~\cite{ng2023}. The farms take a story from a mainstream outlet, have a language model reword it, and publish it as their own. NewsGuard now counts 3,749 AI content-farm sites~\cite{ng2026}. The checker fails for a structural reason: it looks for text copied verbatim, and a paraphrase has very little.

A web crawler has the same blind spot. Its ``content seen?'' check fingerprints each page and compares sets of word shingles, which catches mirrors and light edits~\cite{broder1997,manku2007,iir2008}. A paraphrase shares almost no word 5-grams with its source: in our tests MinHash Jaccard reaches an F1 of 0.021 on SEO-style rewrites (Section~\ref{sec:eval}). So the crawler indexes the rewrite as new content, and in search the copy competes with its own source.

We had two users in mind:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a crawler or search operator who wants to know, when a page arrives, whether it is derived from a stored page, which one, and how to keep the original above its copies;
  \item an editor or fact-checker with one suspicious article who wants its likely source and the evidence: aligned sentences and shared names, numbers and quotes.
\end{itemize}

Track~4 asks for a focused crawler and a small index that address one integrity problem end to end, with the content-seen check, near-duplicate detection and politeness as hooks. Its first sample idea is a news crawler that detects near-duplicate and rewritten articles. We started there and changed what counts as ``seen'': SpinTrace extends the content-seen check from copy detection to paraphrase-aware derivative detection. It retrieves a rewrite's likely source using the facts a rewrite rarely changes, then traces provenance and demotes copies in ranking.

We built on Broder's resemblance and containment~\cite{broder1997}, Manku et al.\ on near-duplicates in a production crawler~\cite{manku2007}, the Mercator frontier~\cite{heydon1999}, the textbook treatment of scoring and index elimination~\cite{iir2008}, BM25~\cite{robertson2009} and Sentence-BERT~\cite{reimers2019}.

\section{How we used IR}

Our premise: a rewrite changes the wording but keeps the names, numbers, dates and direct quotes. In IR terms those are high-idf terms and exact phrases. So classical IR (an inverted index, idf, positional phrase queries, a date filter) finds the candidate sources, and a sentence-embedding model only checks the few candidates IR returns. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the pipeline, and Table~\ref{tab:concepts} groups the concepts under the assignment's five lecture topics. The paragraphs below take each stage in turn, with the reason for each choice.

\paragraph{Crawl (\code{crawl/})} The crawler follows Mercator's split between front queues for priority and back queues for politeness~\cite{heydon1999}. Each host keeps three priority levels (feed and sitemap entries, article-shaped links, the rest) drawn 8:3:1, and a heap of next-allowed fetch times picks the host. Priority sits in one replaceable function, \code{priority()}.

Politeness is enforced twice. robots.txt is fetched per host and obeyed, including Crawl-delay, and requests to one host are at least 5\,s apart, checked by the heap and again by a guard before every request. A 5xx, a 429 or a refusal doubles the delay, Retry-After is honoured, and a host is dropped after 5 consecutive errors. Redirect targets go back through robots.txt and the delay.

URLs are normalised (host lowercased, fragments and tracking parameters dropped, query sorted), and trap guards reject long, deep, repetitive or parameter-heavy URLs and cap a host at 1,500 pages. A document is trafilatura's main text of at least 120 words plus a publish date from metadata or the feed; the date drives provenance.

\paragraph{Text and index (\code{text.py}, \code{index.py})} One normalisation pipeline serves the index, the shingles and the queries: NFKC, case and accent folding, and numbers kept as values so that ``1,394'' matches ``1394''. Stopwords stay in the postings because phrase queries need every position. The index is positional, with title, body and quote zones, and computes lnc.ltc tf-idf and, outside the syllabus, BM25.

\paragraph{Content-seen check (\code{dedup.py})} This is the textbook check we extend, and our main baseline~\cite{broder1997,iir2008}. It hashes the normalised text, then compares word 5-gram shingles with MinHash (128 permutations, estimate error about 0.09) and LSH (32 bands of 4 rows, knee at Jaccard 0.42, so light edits are caught). It still runs first in the crawler.

\paragraph{Rare-term candidate query (\code{detect/candidates.py})} This is the core of SpinTrace: index elimination~\cite[ch.~7]{iir2008}, where only the postings of high-idf terms are walked. The query is the suspect's 30 highest-weight body terms with df ≥ 2, with names and numbers boosted by 2; a term with df = 1 can only match the suspect itself. Scores accumulate term at a time and a heap returns the top 20.

We score with lnc.ltc because it puts idf on the query side only, so the rare terms decide, and cosine length normalisation stops long articles beating a short summary. Quotes are cut into 6-token windows (step 3) and run as phrase queries, since a rewrite may trim a quote and any intact window is evidence. A parametric filter keeps only documents from other sites published before the suspect.

\paragraph{Verification (\code{detect/signals.py}, \code{verify.py})} The top 5 candidates are checked by independent signals. Sentence alignment (two sentences match at MiniLM cosine ≥ 0.62) gives coverage, mean score, order and back coverage of the candidate, plus word overlap inside aligned pairs. Fact signals add quote overlap, name and number overlap, shingle containment and the rare-term cosine. A class-balanced logistic regression fit on dev pairs combines them, thresholded for the best dev F1.

\paragraph{Provenance, g(d) and net score (\code{detect/pipeline.py}, \code{scan.py}, \code{search.py})} The earlier publish date is the source. Without a date, coverage asymmetry decides (margin 0.2), because a summary covers less of its source than the source covers of it; otherwise the answer is ``uncertain''.

Verified edges form a copy graph, and the static quality g(d) = 1 − p(strongest source edge), so originals keep g~=~1 and confirmed copies fall towards 0. Search ranks by \textit{net}(q,\,d)~=~\textit{relevance}(q,\,d)~+~λ\,g(d) over weighted title and body zones. λ was chosen on dev queries from \{0, 0.1, \ldots, 0.8\}; the tuned value, 0.1, lets g(d) break ties between an original and its copies without overriding relevance.

\section{Beyond IR}

\paragraph{Sentence embeddings} all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (sentence-transformers~\cite{reimers2019}) maps sentences to vectors so a paraphrased sentence lands near its source. It has one job: aligning a suspect with the five candidates IR returns.

The ablations (Appendix, Table~\ref{tab:ablations}) show that IR and embeddings need each other. Keeping only the IR signals drops macro F1 from 0.896 to 0.802 and raises the facts-only false-positive rate from 0.156 to 0.444; trusting retrieval alone gives 0.595. Dense-retrieval candidates instead of IR give 0.894, so IR is not more accurate as a candidate generator here. What it adds is readable evidence (which rare terms and quotes matched) and more sources ranked first: 0.993 against 0.961, with dense retrieval at only 0.83 on SEO rewrites and summaries.

\paragraph{Learned combination and test data} A scikit-learn logistic regression combines the ten signals; we chose it for its few parameters and readable weights (\code{results/verifier.json}). Meta Llama 3.2 3B, run locally through Ollama, wrote the SEO rewrites, summaries, round trips through German and facts-only articles, which are evaluation data only.

\paragraph{Libraries, in IR terms} Every IR component in Table~\ref{tab:concepts} is our own code; libraries do the surrounding work. \code{requests} is the fetcher in the crawl loop, and \code{urllib.robotparser} parses robots.txt rules, while caching, delays and backoff are ours. \code{trafilatura} does boilerplate removal, which decides what a document is, and \code{lxml} parses links, RSS, Atom and sitemaps for the frontier. \code{nltk} supplies the Porter stemmer for the stemming comparison (and WordNet, only to build synonym test rewrites). \code{sentence-transformers} maps sentences to vectors for alignment and the dense baseline; \code{scikit-learn} fits the logistic combination of signals and supplies the stopword list; \code{numpy} holds postings arrays. \code{mwparserfromhell} strips wikitext from the Wikinews dump and \code{warcio} reads the CC-NEWS file, both deciding what a document is for those sources.

\paragraph{Outside the syllabus} Two methods go beyond the lectures. BM25~\cite{robertson2009} (\textit{k}\textsubscript{1}~=~1.2, \textit{b}~=~0.75, in \code{index.py}) is a full-document baseline; it comes within 0.05 of SpinTrace on summaries (0.852 against 0.902). Dense retrieval over mean MiniLM sentence vectors (\code{eval/run.py}) is both a detection baseline and an alternative candidate generator; it ranks the true source first for 0.961 of positives against 0.993 for our rare-term query, and it flags half the crawl hard negatives (0.502). Together they answer when sparse retrieval wins.

Beyond the tools in Table~\ref{tab:existing}, the obvious baseline for this track is a shingle near-duplicate detector over a crawl. SpinTrace differs from it in four ways.

Its candidate sources come from the facts a rewrite keeps, as an index-elimination query over the crawler's own index, not from surface overlap or from embedding everything. Its verdicts have a direction: the earlier article is the source, or, without dates, the one that covers the other. Those verdicts feed back into ranking through g(d), so detection changes what users see. And we test it both where the baseline breaks (graded rewrites) and where a paraphrase detector is most likely wrong: independent articles on the same event, which share names and numbers without being copies.

Every component is textbook IR; what is new is combining them at the crawler's content-seen step and measuring where the result fails. A GitHub API search in October 2026 found blocklists and generic plagiarism checkers, but no project combining a crawler, paraphrase-aware detection and provenance tracing.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Data} Positives are rewrites of 400 Wikinews originals at six intensities: exact copy, light edit, WordNet synonym spin, an LLM ``SEO-friendly'' rewrite like NewsGuard's, an LLM summary, and an LLM round trip through German. Negatives are the originals, crawl hard negatives (same event, another outlet, within 3 days, at least 3 shared rare names, no shingle containment above 0.1; median gap 10.5\,h, 77\% within 24\,h), and facts-only articles an LLM wrote from an original's topic, names, numbers and quotes without seeing its text. Every item is scored against all 20,860 documents (crawl, originals, 4,000 Wikinews distractors, all rewrites), except that a rewrite cannot see other rewrites of its own original.

\paragraph{Protocol} We split by original story, so a story and its rewrites fall on one side. Every threshold is tuned on dev, and every number below is from the test split: 190 items each of exact, light and synonym, 90 SEO, 90 summary and 45 translation, against 190 originals, 301 hard negatives and 90 facts-only articles. The baselines are exact hash, MinHash Jaccard over LSH candidates, shingle containment against every earlier document (the best case for shingles), full-document tf-idf cosine, full-document BM25 normalised by the suspect's self-score, and dense cosine of mean MiniLM vectors, each at its best dev threshold.

Shingle checks collapse as soon as the wording changes; SpinTrace degrades slowly and has the best F1 at every level after light edits (Table~\ref{tab:f1}, Figure~\ref{fig:f1}). MinHash Jaccard falls from 0.990 on light edits to 0.322 on synonym spins and 0.021 on SEO rewrites, and exact hash scores 0 after any edit. It loses at the easy end. Exact hash (0.995) and MinHash (0.992, 0.990) beat its 0.960 on exact copies and light edits at almost no cost, because its threshold is tuned for paraphrases and the same-story negatives it flags cost precision (0.922) at every level. In a crawler the two should run together: the textbook check first, SpinTrace for what it lets through. BM25 comes within 0.05 on summaries (0.852), and full-document tf-idf flags slightly fewer facts-only articles (14.4\% against 15.6\%) while missing far more rewrites. On crawl hard negatives SpinTrace's false-positive rate is 0.007; dense cosine flags half of them (0.502).

\subsection{Ranking the original first}

Ranking the right source is easy for sparse methods on this corpus, so it does not separate the methods. SpinTrace ranks the true source first for 0.998 of test positives (MRR 0.998, Recall@20 1.000); full-document BM25 reaches 0.996, tf-idf cosine 0.993, shingle containment 0.987 and dense cosine 0.961. MinHash (0.526) and exact hash (0.239) only rank sources they can detect. The case for the rare-term query is its cost.

\subsection{Human judgments of live flags}

Live precision is a range, because we disagreed. Both of us labelled all 174 pairs SpinTrace flagged in the live crawl, blind to its score, its verdict and each other's label. Precision depends on how the labels combine:
\begin{itemize}
  \item 0.845 if either judge says derived (our convention, and the lenient one);
  \item 0.738 on the 103 pairs we agreed on;
  \item 0.437 if both must say derived.
\end{itemize}
We agreed on 59\% of pairs (Cohen's kappa 0.123); Judge~1 called 103 derived and Judge~2 called 120. On 154 search results agreement was 0.636 (kappa 0.161). Judge~1's not-derived pairs have much less exact overlap (median shingle containment 0.38 against 0.85), which fits sentence alignment matching independent coverage of one event.

\subsection{Documented farm pairs}

Two pairs are anecdotes, not a measurement. Two of four NewsGuard-documented pairs loaded from the Wayback Machine (the report does not name the other originals). The New York Times original of a GlobalVillageSpace rewrite is retrieved first among 20,860 documents, verified (p~=~0.999) and traced. The Wired original of a TopGolf.kr page is also retrieved first but not verified (p~=~0.0): that page is AI title suggestions plus a reshaped deals list, and our verifier was trained on prose.

\section{Limitations and next steps}

\paragraph{Same story versus copied} This is the weakest boundary. Articles written from an original's facts and quotes alone are still flagged 15.6\% of the time, and recall on SEO rewrites, the kind NewsGuard tested, is 0.76 at the dev-tuned threshold.

\paragraph{Wire syndication, not farms} The crawl contained no actual farm sites. The live flags are mostly wire syndication (PTI, ANI, Reuters), credited to whichever outlet we crawled first because agencies are not crawled. Our only real farm cases are the two NewsGuard pairs.

\paragraph{Quotes turned out neutral} Copies and independent coverage quote the same speakers. Retrieval finds the source first for 0.993 of positives with quote phrases and 0.994 without, and removing quote overlap leaves macro F1 at 0.896. Names and numbers carry the evidence: without them macro F1 is 0.884 and the facts-only false-positive rate 0.222. Same-event pairs that share quotes are not yet tested.

\paragraph{Other limits} Judge agreement was low (kappa 0.123 on live flags, 0.161 on search), so live precision is a range from 0.437 to 0.845. The synthetic rewrites come from one 3B model; stronger models paraphrase more. Dates are trusted, so a farm that backdates its pages escapes the earlier-only filter; dropping the filter costs little in retrieval (Recall@1 0.989 against 0.993), but undated provenance mostly abstains except on summaries (Appendix, Table~\ref{tab:undated}). Scale is modest: one crawler process and 32.5k articles over 13.5 hours, of which the evaluation used about the first 16k.

\paragraph{Course-project roadmap} We plan to continue SpinTrace as our course project, in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Farm-aware focused crawling: raise the priority of domains with high derivative rates through the \code{priority()} hook.
  \item Recognise agency datelines and bylines, so syndicated copies point to the agency rather than to the first outlet crawled.
  \item A harder evaluation: rewrites from stronger LLMs, same-event negatives that share quotes, and an external benchmark (PAN~\cite{potthast2010} or METER).
  \item Signal weights learned from judged live pairs, after a second labelling round with a tighter guide.
  \item Adaptive recrawl scheduling, more fetcher threads and a larger CC-NEWS index; later, English-to-Hindi derivatives.
\end{enumerate}
